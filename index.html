How the week runs

Five sessions, A to E, done in order on whatever days suit you. You keep the full-body feel, but each session leans on different muscles so every set is done fresh. Legs, abs and biceps get hit most because they're the priority.

  • Rest days are yours to place. Two a week. Don't train more than 3 days in a row, and the app flags it if you try.
  • Missed a day? Do the next session in the order. Don't skip ahead.
MuscleHard sets / weekSessions
  • 2 working sets per exercise, after 1–3 ramp-up sets on the first lift.
  • Failure rules: machines, cables and isolations go to true failure. Squat, RDL, bench and hack squat stop at 1 rep in reserve (RIR).
  • Progression: once you hit the top of the rep range on both sets with clean form, add weight (2.5 kg barbell, next dumbbell up) and drop back to the bottom of the range.
  • Rest between sets: 2–3 min on compounds, 60–90 s on isolations. Superset abs and lateral raises with something else to save time.
  • Deload every 6–8 weeks, or if your lifts drop two sessions running: one week, 1 set per exercise at about 3 RIR.
  • About 50–55 minutes including a 5-minute bike warm-up.

Making it easier to eat

  • Eat on the clock: 8am, 12pm, 3pm, 7pm, 9:30pm. Don't wait to be hungry.
  • Drink calories. Whole milk, orange juice and Tom's shake go down when food won't.
  • Keep veg as a side, not a pile. It fills you up without many calories.
  • If the scale hasn't moved after 2 weeks, add 150–200 kcal (an extra 30 g of peanut butter, or 40 g more rice per tub).
  • Food safety: cool rice and meat within an hour and keep them in the fridge. Eat the Sunday tubs within 3 days. Freeze the last 2 days of the Wednesday batch, defrost in the fridge the night before, and reheat until steaming.

Why this setup

  • Volume over everything. Muscle growth tracks weekly hard sets. Around 10–20 per muscle works well for trained lifters, so priority muscles sit at the top of that range (Schoenfeld 2017; Pelland 2024).
  • Your 2 sets to failure is fine. Low-set training works if weekly volume is there. Going to failure vs. stopping 1–2 reps short gives similar growth, but failure on heavy compounds costs more fatigue, so those stop at 1 RIR (Refalo 2023; Robinson 2024).
  • Why not pure full body. At equal volume, frequency barely matters once each muscle is hit twice a week (Schoenfeld 2016, 2019). Rotating the focus means the last exercise of each session isn't done exhausted.
  • Stretch bias. Exercises loaded at long muscle lengths tend to grow muscle a bit better: incline and Bayesian curls, overhead triceps, RDLs, seated leg curls (Maeo 2021, 2023).
  • Long rests. 2–3 minutes between hard sets gives better hypertrophy than short rests (Schoenfeld 2016; Singer 2024).
  • Protein. 1.6–2.2 g/kg covers it (Morton 2018). Both of you are set at about 2 g/kg or more.
  • Rate of gain. For a lean bulk aim for 0.25–0.5% of bodyweight a week, about 0.2–0.4 kg (Iraki 2019).
  • Creatine. 3–5 g every day, any time, no loading needed. It's the best-supported supplement for strength and size (ISSN position stand, Kreider 2017).

How targets were set

Mifflin-St Jeor BMR × 1.6 activity, plus a surplus. Tom: about 3,050 maintenance, so 3,500 kcal, extra because tall, lean lifters usually burn more than the formula predicts. Joe: about 2,920 maintenance, so 3,150 kcal. These are starting points. Adjust by the scale every 2 weeks.
